# Connection through platform adapters

Authorizing a destination and keeping its credential alive are routed through the same platform adapter that publishes to it, resolved from the one registry, so a platform is connectable exactly when it is publishable. The adapter owns what needs platform knowledge: the credential it asks the creator for, the exchange that validates it and reports the identity behind it, the rule for when that credential may be rolled forward, and how to read a failure as expired or revoked. Everything around those calls stays platform-neutral — which connection ID a destination gets, what the stored connection records, the order in which the secret and the connection are persisted, and when a refresh is due — so the connect screen renders whatever credential an adapter declares and the shell drops into its reconnect state without naming a platform. Identity is read as a neutral handle, name, audience count, and avatar; the legacy Instagram-shaped account survives only as a transitional mapping in the shell.
